## 1. Executive summary

**The operator's hypothesis is not supported by the data on this historical window.**

Across **7,932 production-equivalent BB Bounce entries** generated by the deployed `strategy.py Strategy.on_candle` state machine over **2024-01-02 → 2026-03-31**, measured with chronological tick-level BID/ASK execution:

* Only **16.1 %** of eventual +40-pip reversals had maximum adverse excursion below 4 pips before reaching +40p.  Only **25.9 %** stayed within 6 pips.  Median pre-success MAE on +40p reversals = **12.7 pips** — more than 3× the deployed 4-pip IG minimum.
* **77.98 %** of eventual +40p reversals revisited the C1 rejection extreme before reaching +40p — i.e. the pattern typically dips below the pierce low (BUY) or above the pierce high (SELL) before the real move.
* A full 30-cell (6 stops × 5 targets) grid shows **every cell is negative-expectancy** on the raw strategy.  The "least bad" cell is **S12 / T60** (production stop, wider target): net −8,381 pips, expectancy −1.057 p/trade, PF 0.889.
* The tightest configurations are **worse** than the production stop, not better: S4/T30 → −9,185 p, PF 0.682; S4/T60 → −8,521 p, PF 0.714.
* Chronological **DEV / UNSEEN split (2026-01-01 cutoff)** shows a **Spearman ρ of −0.362** between the two periods' expectancy rankings — the "best stop" is unstable.  Any apparent improvement in a single sub-period is almost certainly overfit.
* Measured entry slippage from Phase 5 (mean 0.65 p, worst 3.35 p) is **large enough to erase any marginal stop-choice gain**.  At +1 pip adverse slippage, every cell is at PF ≤ 0.79; at +3.5 pip adverse, PF collapses toward 0 for 4-pip stops.
* IG DEMO's minimum normal stop on GBP/USD is **4.0 points** (= 4.0 pips, captured 2026-09-27 `dealing_rules.json`).  The lowest historically accepted stop in the Phase 4 broker ledger was **4.5 pips**.  Both numbers are confirmed; they do not establish that 4-pip stops are the right economic choice.

**Recommendation: do NOT run a controlled DEMO test of tight stops as a cost-reduction mechanism on the raw BB Bounce.**  The data falsifies the economic premise.  A productive next direction would be an **entry filter** that identifies the ~16 % of ≥ 40 p movers with sub-4-pip MAE and takes only them — but that is a different research question, not a stop-tightening exercise, and must be designed and tested with equivalent dev / unseen discipline.

## 3. Pre-registered design (frozen before outcome analysis)

See `PROGRESS.md` — written before Stage 5 outcomes were inspected.

* **Historical window:** 2024-01-01 → 2026-03-21 (Phase 2 corrected tick cache, 52.99 M rows across three parquet files).
* **DEV / UNSEEN cutoff:** 2026-01-01.
* **Signal source:** deployed `/opt/bb_bounce/strategy.py Strategy.on_candle` driven on 5-min MID candles (OHLC in points, rounded 2 dp) reconstructed from BID/ASK ticks.
* **Entry fill:** first tick strictly after C3.close_time (= C3.ts + 5 min), max wait 60 s. BUY = ASK, SELL = BID.
* **Exits:** BUY stop/target measured against subsequent BID; SELL against subsequent ASK.
* **Horizon:** min(24 h after entry, Friday 21:00 UTC of the fill-week).
* **Targets:** 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 / 60 pips.
* **Fixed stops:** 4 / 5 / 6 / 8 / 10 / 12 pips.
* **Structural buffers:** 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3 pips below (BUY) / above (SELL) min(C1,C2) extreme.
* **Metrics:** winners, losers, win-rate, avg win / loss, gross / net pips, expectancy, PF, max DD, consecutive losers, original-winners-stopped, loser-cost-reduction.
* **Broker admissibility scenarios:** A/B/C/D/E as per §2.9.

## 9. Final recommendation

**Do not deploy tighter stops as a stand-alone change.**  The historical evidence directly contradicts the economic premise:

* 84 % of eventual +40-pip winners would be killed by a 4-pip stop.
* The 16 % that survive do not generate enough favourable pips to offset the lost 84 % plus the losing trades' residual cost.
* Dev / unseen anti-correlation (ρ = −0.362) means a stop choice optimised on 2024-25 would likely be the WRONG choice for 2026.
* Realistic slippage (0.5–3.5 p adverse, Phase 5) widens the gap between configurations and dominates stop choice.

If the operator remains interested in tight-stop operation, the next step is **not** a DEMO run of S4/T30 — it is a controlled research study that attempts to identify the ~16 % of entries where tight stops survive by design (e.g. rejection-candle size, trend-regime filters, prior-day structure proximity).  That is a Phase 7 scope.

**No production trading is modified.  No tight-stop deployment is proposed.**
